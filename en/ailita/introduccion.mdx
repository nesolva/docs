Ailita ID is one place to access your applications and look after your personal information. It's where you sign in and where you manage your account. You use the same account to sign in to the Nesolva products your organisation uses, such as Alfred.

## What you can do with your account

- Sign in to your organisation's applications. See [Sign in](/en/ailita/iniciar-sesion).
- Create your account when your organisation invites you or allows sign-up. See [Create your account](/en/ailita/crear-cuenta).
- Look after your details in **My account**: your name, your phone and your email. See [Personal details](/en/ailita/cuenta/datos-personales).
- Protect your account with an authenticator app, backup codes, passkeys and a password. See [Security](/en/ailita/cuenta/seguridad).
- Decide which applications use your account and what they can see. See [Connected apps](/en/ailita/cuenta/aplicaciones).
- Review where you signed in and end sessions you don't recognise. See [Devices and sessions](/en/ailita/cuenta/dispositivos-y-sesiones) and [Activity](/en/ailita/cuenta/actividad).

## What depends on your organisation

Your account belongs to an organisation, and it decides how you sign in. The sign-in screen shows your organisation's logo, name and colour.

<Note>
If your organisation allows it, you can sign in with a password, with an email code, with a passkey or with your Google or Microsoft account. If your organisation requires it, you also need a second factor. If an option in this help doesn't appear on your screen, your organisation hasn't turned it on.
</Note>

What doesn't depend on your organisation: you can review and change your details, your security and your sessions from **My account**.

## If something goes wrong

- Forgot your password? See [Forgot your password](/en/ailita/recuperar/contrasena).
- Lost your phone and your backup codes? See [I lost my second factor](/en/ailita/recuperar/segundo-factor).
- Seeing an error message when you sign in? See [I can't sign in](/en/ailita/problemas/iniciar-sesion).
