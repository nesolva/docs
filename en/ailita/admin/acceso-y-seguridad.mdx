In **Access & security** you decide how the people in your organisation see and use Ailita: the look of the sign-in screen, the sign-in methods and the session rules.

<Note>
  When you save, the Backoffice tells you the changes apply from the next sign-in. People who already have an open session won't notice them until they sign in again.
</Note>

## Branding

In **Branding** you set your organisation's name, the address of its logo, the primary colour and the default language. Your people's sign-in screen shows the logo, the name and the colour.

## How people sign in

### Sign-in method

| Option | What your people see |
|---|---|
| **Password** | They sign in with their email and password. |
| **Password or email code** | Besides the password, they can ask us to email them a code (**Email me a code instead**). |
| **Corporate SSO only** | They go straight to Microsoft or Google to sign in with their work account. First you need to [verify your domain and set up SSO](/en/ailita/admin/dominios-y-sso). |

### Account creation

- **Invitation only**: people can create their account only with an [invitation](/en/ailita/admin/usuarios-e-invitaciones). Anyone who tries without one sees "This organisation only accepts invited users."
- **Open**: anyone can create their account from the sign-in screen with **Create account**.

### Options you turn on or off

- **Passkeys**: if they're on, your people see **Sign in with a passkey** and can add passkeys to their account. If you turn them off, they see "Your organisation doesn't allow passkeys."
- **Authenticator app**: lets people use a code from an app as a second factor.
- **Password recovery by email**: shows the **Forgot your password?** link on the sign-in screen.

## Sessions and friction

- **Second factor required for everyone**: if you turn it on, anyone without a second factor sees **Protect your account** and sets one up before continuing. Administrators always need a second factor, whether or not this option is on.
- Maximum session length: how long a session can last in total.
- Idle timeout: how long without activity before the session is closed.
- How often to ask for the second factor again.
- Remember device.
- Maximum concurrent sessions per person.

Choose the values that best fit how your organisation works. The shorter the sessions, the more often your people have to sign in.
